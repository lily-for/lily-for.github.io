01 / ASME MAKE-A-THON

NeuroSense

Adaptive sensory feedback glove

3rd place · 2026

The Make-a-Thon challenge

During a 48-hour University of Iowa Make-a-Thon organized by ASME, SWE, and 3D Club, our five-person team explored how a wearable could help people with reduced hand sensation recognize unsafe temperatures and judge grip pressure.

Our prototype

NeuroSense combines five fingertip pressure sensors and five thermistors with a Teensy 4.0. Each resistive pressure sensor uses a voltage-divider circuit to translate changes in fingertip force into an analog reading. Those readings drive a four-LED grip display: more pressure lights more LEDs, giving the wearer a visual cue for how firmly they are holding an object.

The thermistors provide temperature readings for an RGB indicator—blue for cold, green for safe, and red for hot—with buzzer alerts for unsafe temperatures. We developed the glove through breadboard testing and custom 3D-printed fingertip sensor holders.

My contribution

  • Helped build the breadboard circuit for the glove prototype.
  • Defined temperature-alert thresholds using skin injury research.
  • Specified the LED and buzzer warning behavior.
  • Tested Teensy-based thermistor feedback under hot and cold conditions, checking the alerts against the expected thresholds.

03 / INTERNET OF THINGS COURSE

Star Sniper

Smart telescope prototype

The project

Star Sniper is a smart telescope prototype developed for our Internet of Things course. The goal was to let a user select a star and have the telescope aim toward it, combining an adjustable physical platform with the broader pointing system.

The base used two stages of adjustment: three linear actuators for coarse leveling and three servos intended for finer changes to the telescope platform. The fine-leveling system was inspired by the screw adjustments used to level a 3D printer bed.

My contribution

  • Built the tripod and assembled the adjustable platform that supported the telescope.
  • Integrated three linear actuators into the leveling subsystem to provide coarse adjustment.
  • Developed the fine-adjustment setup around three servos, intended to make smaller corrections to the platform.

HARDWARETripod · adjustable platform · linear actuators · servos

04 / ASME STUDENT DESIGN COMPETITION

Autonomous garbage truck

A vehicle for a model-city waste collection challenge

2nd place · 2025–2026

The project

Our team built Big Dumper for the 2025–26 ASME Student Design Competition at EFx in Dallas. Teams had to drive across a model city, collect blocks representing trash and recyclables, and deliver them to the landfill or recycling center.

We built a truck that collected blocks, read their colors with onboard sensors, and directed them into separate compartments for trash and recycling. The build brought together the drive controls, collection hardware, and sorting mechanism. As a team, we tested and refined how those pieces worked together, from picking up a block to keeping the two types of waste separate for drop-off.

My contribution

  • Helped develop the color-sensing setup that distinguished trash blocks from recycling blocks.
  • Contributed to the microcontroller setup for remote driving so the team could maneuver the truck around the course.
  • Helped test and refine the truck with the team as we brought the collection and sorting systems together.

06 / ELECTRONICS / FALL 2026

Infrared Transmitter & Receiver

From a modulated light source to an analog receiver

Bench prototype

The project

A course project building an infrared transmitter and receiver. The signal chain connects an IR LED transmitter to a photodiode receiver, amplification, filtering, and a detector that drives an output.

My work at the bench

  • Iterated the transmitter’s LED arrangement and current-limiting resistor while working toward the course’s 20-foot range target.
  • Measured a 30.4 kHz carrier and an average LED current of 39.55 mA with two IR LEDs in series and a 33 Ω resistor, just below the 40 mA average limit.
  • Troubleshot a receiver built around a photodiode transimpedance stage, a tuned amplifier near 30 kHz, a clamp and peak detector, and an amplified MOSFET / solid-state-relay output.

07 / EMBEDDED SOFTWARE / FALL 2026

MorseKey

Turning a bouncing button into timed Morse events

Team lab project

The project

An ESP32 team lab connecting physical button input with Morse timing and a larger software system. My main contribution was the input layer: recognizing intentional presses and classifying their duration and the silence between them.

My contribution

  • Implemented button debouncing and timing classification in C, using 25 ms debounce and a 200 ms Morse timing unit.
  • Classified dots and dashes at a 400 ms boundary, with letter and word gap events at 600 ms and 1400 ms.
  • Added host-side tests and interface documentation. Worked through button-bounce edge cases with the team, including preserving the intended gap boundaries.

08 / ASSISTIVE TECHNOLOGY / IN PROGRESS

IMU gait exploration

Making motion data useful for an exoskeleton project

Early exploration

The project

An early-stage team project with a basic exoskeleton frame and IMUs on a breadboard. The frame is passive at this stage; the current work is about understanding motion data before adding active assistance.

My focus

  • Investigating how IMU data can support gait prediction, starting with one sensor above the ankle and one on the thigh.
  • Exploring which motion features could help identify what the wearer is doing and what happens next.
  • Considering the time between reading the sensors and making a useful decision. Prediction accuracy and response latency still need to be established.
