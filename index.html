05 / SENIOR DESIGN / 2026–2027

Rescue Rover

Affordable reconnaissance for hazardous industrial spaces

In progress

The project

A five-person senior-design project exploring a battery-powered, remotely operated rover that could help responders assess factories and warehouses before entering a hazardous area. Affordability and modular sensing are central to the concept.

My involvement

  • Working with a team of three computer science / computer engineering students and two electrical engineering students to develop the rover concept.
  • Helped shape and communicate the project’s affordability and mission scope for a funding pitch.
  • The proposed system combines mapping, visual and thermal cameras, and environmental sensing in one operator view. These are design goals for the prototype, with controlled-environment testing still ahead.

Funding & next steps

We’re seeking funding to build and test the prototype. Our team pitched Rescue Rover at the JPEC Innovation Challenge in November 2026. We did not receive a cash award and are continuing to look for funding opportunities.

06 / ELECTRONICS / FALL 2026

Infrared Transmitter & Receiver

From a modulated light source to an analog receiver

Bench prototype

The project

A course project building an infrared transmitter and receiver. The signal chain connects an IR LED transmitter to a photodiode receiver, amplification, filtering, and a detector that drives an output.

My work at the bench

  • Iterated the transmitter’s LED arrangement and current-limiting resistor while working toward the course’s 20-foot range target.
  • Measured a 30.4 kHz carrier and an average LED current of 39.55 mA with two IR LEDs in series and a 33 Ω resistor, just below the 40 mA average limit.
  • Troubleshot a receiver built around a photodiode transimpedance stage, a tuned amplifier near 30 kHz, a clamp and peak detector, and an amplified MOSFET / solid-state-relay output.

07 / EMBEDDED SOFTWARE / FALL 2026

MorseKey

Turning a bouncing button into timed Morse events

Team lab project

The project

An ESP32 team lab connecting physical button input with Morse timing and a larger software system. My main contribution was the input layer: recognizing intentional presses and classifying their duration and the silence between them.

My contribution

  • Implemented button debouncing and timing classification in C, using 25 ms debounce and a 200 ms Morse timing unit.
  • Classified dots and dashes at a 400 ms boundary, with letter and word gap events at 600 ms and 1400 ms.
  • Added host-side tests and interface documentation. Worked through button-bounce edge cases with the team, including preserving the intended gap boundaries.

WORKED WITH / FOCUSESP32 · ESP-IDF · C · host-side tests · GitLab · interface documentation

INPUT

08 / ASSISTIVE TECHNOLOGY / IN PROGRESS

IMU gait exploration

Making motion data useful for an exoskeleton project

Early exploration

The project

An early-stage team project with a basic exoskeleton frame and IMUs on a breadboard. The frame is passive at this stage; the current work is about understanding motion data before adding active assistance.

My focus

  • Investigating how IMU data can support gait prediction, starting with one sensor above the ankle and one on the thigh.
  • Exploring which motion features could help identify what the wearer is doing and what happens next.
  • Considering the time between reading the sensors and making a useful decision. Prediction accuracy and response latency still need to be established.
